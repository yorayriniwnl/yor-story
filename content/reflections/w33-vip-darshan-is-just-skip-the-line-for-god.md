There are two queues at the temple. One is free. One costs a thousand rupees. God is at the front of both. The thousand-rupee queue gets there in twelve minutes. The free queue: three to five hours, depending on the festival, the season, how many buses came from which city.

I need you to sit with what this means. Not symbolically. Mechanically.

> The deity — who is, according to the theology that built these temples, omnipresent, omniscient, and entirely beyond the material plane — has been organized into a priority access system. The sacred has a fast lane.

And the people running the temple trust figured out what every airline, every concert venue, every luxury hotel already understood: the product is not the destination. The product is the wait. Eliminate the wait and you have created a premium tier. The devotion is the same on both sides of the rope. The air conditioning is not.

Both queues have explained this to themselves. The thousand-rupee devotees: "I give to the temple. The money goes to the institution." The free devotees: "I have patience and patience is devotion." Both explanations are self-serving. Both are probably sincere. Both are wrong about what they are actually buying.

What they are buying, in both cases, is access to a representation. Not God. A murti. A specific idol in a specific temple in a specific city — one of hundreds of representations of the same deity across the country, each equally valid according to the theology. The omnipresent one does not have a preferred physical address. The trust does.

---

VIP darshan is not a corruption of religion. It is religion operating exactly as it was designed — as an institution, with institutional economics, institutional class logic, and institutional interest in maintaining both. The cancer metaphor is not about the money. The money is honest. The cancer is the silence around it. The seamless coexistence of transcendence and ticketing, and the fact that nobody is even slightly embarrassed.

Walk past the free queue next time. Five hours. Children sitting on the ground. Elderly people in the sun. All of them believing they are in the presence of something beyond all human hierarchy. Standing in a hierarchy that God apparently signed off on.
