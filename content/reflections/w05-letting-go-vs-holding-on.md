Everyone applauds the person who lets go. The songs are written for them. The philosophy is on their side. "True love means wanting the best for them." "If you love something, set it free." It is the most romantically valorized act in the entire vocabulary of relationships. The nobility of the open hand.

Nobody talks about what it costs.

Letting go — when you mean it, when it is not relief dressed as nobility, when you are not secretly glad to be done — is not peace. It is a decision you make once and then make again every day until the feeling becomes something else. You wake up and it costs. You see something and want to tell her and it costs. You are fine for three weeks and then something small breaks the surface and it costs. There is no lump-sum payment for letting go. It is an installment plan with no final payment date.

> The person who lets go because they love someone enough — who absorbs the full cost, who says "this is better for you and I will carry what that means" — is the hero of a private story that nobody else will read. The public story needs a reunion or a tragedy. Quiet, daily, dignified sacrifice fits neither format.

The phrase has been aestheticized into something clean. "Loved her enough to let her go." It sounds like a resolution. It is not a resolution. It is the beginning of a very long, very private accounting. The person who says this sentence — if they mean it — is not at the end of something. They are at the beginning of living inside the consequences of the most expensive decision love ever asked them to make.

And they made it. Because that is what it looked like from where they were standing. Not because it was easy. Not because they are enlightened. Because the other option was holding on past the point where holding on was honest, and they could not do that to her.

---

I am not telling you not to let go. Sometimes it is the only honest thing. Sometimes it is the only way love stays love instead of becoming ownership.

I am telling you: stop calling it beautiful. Call it what it is. The most brutal thing love ever asked you to do. You did it. You are still doing it. That deserves a more accurate name than letting go.
