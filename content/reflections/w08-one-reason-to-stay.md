Not asking you to be ashamed of where you are from. That is not this. I am asking for a ledger. Honest accounting. No sentimentality, no "but the culture" as a category exempt from examination.

Column one: what India gives you that you actually want. Not what you are told to be grateful for. Not what exists in every country. What this specific place, with this specific system, gives a person actually trying to live honestly in it.

Column two: what it takes.

> I have been running this ledger for years. Column two is longer every time. Not because I am ungrateful. Because I am accurate.

Column two: the air in the cities. The traffic that is not a metaphor for anything — it is just traffic, it kills people, and nothing structurally changes. The casteism that does not appear in the data shown to foreign investors but shows up in every room the moment you know what to look for. The bureaucratic deflection that has been refined over seventy years into an art form — the English of official correspondence is always the English of "your application is under review." The parents who sacrificed genuinely and use it as currency for the rest of your life. The weddings. The ambient noise of other people's opinions about your choices. The gap between what the country says it is and what it demonstrably is.

Column one has things in it. I will name them honestly. The food, which is a genuine argument that holds up under examination. The density of human relationship, which is real and occasionally sustaining. The humor that only works in this specific combination of languages — the kind that exists in the gap between what is said in English and what is meant in Hindi and what is felt in the silence after. Some of the art. The sense, sometimes, of being embedded in something much older and stranger than you, which is occasionally useful and occasionally just overwhelming.

---

But column one keeps being used to silence column two. And that is the transaction I am done with. Every time someone says "but the culture," what they mean is stop asking column-two questions. Culture is not an answer to infrastructure failure. Tradition is not an excuse for institutional rot. The biryani is incredible. The biryani is not a rebuttal.

Give me one reason that makes sense. Not a feeling. A reason. Something that holds its shape when you look at it for more than five minutes. I am still looking. The ledger is still open. I report what I find.
