The soul cannot feel pain. Every major Hindu theological tradition says this explicitly. The Gita says it directly — the atma is indestructible, immutable, beyond physical sensation. What you feel is the body. The body dies. The atma does not. The atma cannot be cut, burned, drowned, or dried. It transcends all sensation.

So explain hell to me. Not metaphorically. Mechanically.

> If the soul has no nerve endings — and it cannot, because it has no physical substrate — then what is the punishment in hell operating on? The body is dead. You left it at the cremation ground. What is burning?

This is not a rhetorical question. It is the load-bearing column of the entire guilt architecture. Pull it and the building falls.

Hell works because of the credible threat of pain after death. It keeps people in line across millennia, across dozens of cultures, across every version of monotheism and certain strains of dharmic tradition. The mechanism is fear of suffering. But if the thing that suffers has already been disposed of — if the soul, by its own theological definition, is beyond sensation — then the threat has no mechanism. The gun has no bullet. The sentence has no sentence.

Nobody in a temple will answer this directly. The conversation will redirect to karma, to the complexity of scripture, to the limits of human understanding, to interpretations of hell that are more metaphorical — separation from the divine, a state of being rather than a place of torture. All of which is interesting. Some of which is genuinely sophisticated. None of which is what the priest told your grandmother.

---

There are two possibilities. One: the theologians who designed hell did not think this through, which is a problem for the epistemological foundation of the institution. Two: they thought it through and built the inconsistency in deliberately, knowing that fear does not require logical consistency — only emotional credibility.

The soul cannot feel pain. The suggestion box is also on fire. File your answer accordingly.
